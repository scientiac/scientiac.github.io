Losing Identity

2026-11-25 2026-11-25Articles

scientiac

I have been a Linux user ever since I got my own computer. It was also how I managed to differentiate myself from others. It became my identity after a while. Now that I take it for granted, making Linux my identity sounds cliche. But, Linux isn't just an operating system. It is a whole community of people with many different opinions and various skills.

While there are too many things that may fragment this community, there are some things that unite the majority of people together as well. Apart from the actual Linux kernel that united us all, there are some things that are generally accepted as being a normal person in the community.

  • A privacy conscious mindset.
  • A love for customization and options.
  • Some valid hate for big tech.
  • To be somewhat conscious of what's running on our devices, in the foreground or the background.

I have grown up with Linux. I have seen it improve and become more user friendly and capable. I have grown up with the community and have tried my best to fit in. And it taught me various things that I wouldn't have learned if it wasn't for Linux.

I went from fearing the terminal to loving it the most. I learned to piece together multiple tools and automate various tasks in the computer. I learned about privacy, free and open source software, open protocols, cross compatibility and a very faint idea of what a good software and its ecosystem would look like.

While I still don't have any Idea if such architecture and ecosystem can earn while providing value while not getting exploited. I am biased toward using such applications which fulfill my criteria for being good.

In the process of learning how this community functions and what it accepts and what it considers to be the "proper" way of choosing and using computers and its applications. I had decided to be far from big tech unless it was not practical to function in the real world. I was a student so it didn't affect me much.

Now that the student era for me is over, I had to look for proper jobs. And I had to use the whole arsenal of what I was deciding not to use. Starting from LinkedIn, then WhatsApp and finally having to switch to windows since the corporate software doesn't support Linux. And while I was at it I started using Facebook, Instagram and Twitter and even started to feel the population of people using big tech. And that my friends and family are very active in sharing their lives in it. I joined in with them and started being a normal person as well, in the now new community of people.

Going from having pseudonyms and communicating privately via text to sharing my real identity and real pictures and videos. It's taking some effort to adjust to this new environment. I used to spend time customizing my computer and backing up files using git and making the GitHub account timeline look green and active, now in Windows that's all gone, I rarely use git, or even launch the terminal. I just open the web browser, and the corporate software and that's the whole day. I use the "normal" social media and try to look active in it. I share my poetry in video form and just wonder if I could have steered my life a little more differently.

You never know how much the community you interact with influences what kind of person you are in your own mind as well as in other's. I used to judge people for not caring about privacy or what kind of software they are using and how it might be exploiting them. Now, I understand why. In a way I have become a person whom my past self would judge. Doing the same things that I used to say that I would never do.
